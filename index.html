Abstract

Automating peg-transfer, a fundamental laparoscopic surgery training task, on the da Vinci Research Kit (dVRK) has been limited to single-step maneuvers, far short of the multi-step sequences performed in clinical practice. Successful surgical task automation requires not only precise low-level control but also planning across many dependent subtasks. To study this challenge, we introduce the Multi-Ring Transfer Benchmark, which extends peg-transfer into long-horizon rearrangement tasks with controllable planning complexity. Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have shown promise for multi-step robot manipulation from visual observations and high-level instructions, yet they continue to struggle with state tracking, constraint satisfaction, and recovery in long-horizon tasks. Recent Reasoning Models (RMs) introduce explicit reasoning traces, but whether these traces translate into improved robotic task planning remains an open question.

To address this, we present HierarchicalRM, which combines RMs with hierarchical task decomposition to reduce planning depth in long-horizon manipulation. In simulation, HierarchicalRM completes tasks up to 3× more complex than standard VLMs while reducing replanning by 50%. We further validate end-to-end execution on the dVRK, demonstrating recovery from failed grasps through visual verification and replanning. Analysis of thinking tokens reveals that reasoning depth directly correlates with task success, positioning RMs as a promising framework for advancing autonomous surgical planning.

Approach

Architecture Overview

 

The system runs in three stages. In Pre-Planning, a Scene Descriptor parses the scene and the H1/H2 Action Generators build a library of hierarchical actions. In Planning & Correction, a Decision Bot composes an H1/H2 plan from the state and instruction; an Inner Bot validates it and returns targeted fixes. In Execution & Verification, an Action Resolver dispatches actions via the Low-Level Motor Module, the Scene Descriptor updates state, and an Outer Bot verifies progress. Failures trigger replanning until completion or failure.

Ablation Study Across Reasoning Configs

The below table highlights the effectiveness of various modules in our pipeline compared to ReplanVLM baseline. We observe that lack of scene and state descriptors reduces the success rate of the tasks by upto 50% for low reasoning configs while medium reasoning doesn't seem to incur this effect. However, removing the hierarchical actions module causes a significant drop in success rate for both reasoning configs. In addition to a drop in success rate, we also see that replan counts increases significantly in the ablation for hierarchical actions compared to the descriptors.

Abbreviations: SR = Success Rate, RC = Mean Replan Count, F = Full model, -D = w/o scene-state descriptors, -H = w/o hierarchical actions (H1/H2), RVLM = ReplanVLM.
